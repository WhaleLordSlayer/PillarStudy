Topic discovery

A topical guide that can reach beyond exact words.

A printed index works best when you already know its heading. Cultivate keeps the historical headings and references, then adds a second discovery layer that can find passages with related meaning even when the wording is different.

01

Start with a Topic.

Open one of 304 accepted Topics and see the scriptures connected to it in one place.

02

Read the passage.

Every reference opens in the reader, so the Guide stays connected to the scripture text instead of becoming a separate commentary system.

03

Keep following the subject.

Move among related Topics, historical cross-references, scripture passages, and Guide Index entries as your study expands.

Meaning-aware discovery

Scripture does not always use the words you would search for.

Historical indexes, modern readers, and scripture itself can use different vocabulary for the same idea. Cultivate uses semantic mapping to find strong candidate connections by meaning, then publishes only the relationships that pass the project's acceptance process.

The result is a larger scripture discovery layer without rewriting the historical source record.

THE DISCOVERY PROBLEM

Semantic retrieval helps bridge those vocabulary differences.

Historical foundation

It starts with actual indexes, not a generated list of religious topics.

Cultivate reconstructed its historical topical foundation from four frozen Latter-day Saint sources published from 1917 through 1921. The pipeline preserves headings, references, aliases, cross-references, quoted context, and source provenance wherever the source supports them.

Those source-attested relationships remain distinct internally from later semantic relationships. New discovery does not rewrite what the historical sources actually indexed.

What gets published

Discovery is broad. Publication is selective.

A similarity score can find a promising passage. It is not enough by itself to publish that passage under a Topic. Cultivate keeps candidate discovery separate from the accepted data that ships in the app.

Source-attested

4,863 historical relationships

These Topic to verse links come from the frozen historical source corpus and retain their provenance.

Semantic discovery

9,844 accepted high-confidence relationships

These are derived connections that passed the accepted enrichment process. They are not presented internally as something a historical source printed.

Runtime behavior

The app does not invent Topic links while you read.

Accepted relationships are generated and reviewed upstream, committed to a versioned dataset, and shipped to the app as normal data. There is no live vector database deciding what belongs under a Topic.
