Privacy Policy

Effective date: August 6, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Cultivate Study app ("Cultivate Study," "we," "us") collects, how it's used, and your choices. If you have questions, contact us at cultivatestudy@gmail.com.

The short version

Cultivate Study is designed to work on your device. You can read scripture, take notes, highlight, and track your reading without creating an account. If you choose to create an account, your data is synced to our backend so it's available across your devices. We do not sell your data or show ads. We do use a small set of service providers described below (an AI provider for some AI features, and a product-analytics/crash-reporting provider) — neither shows you ads or sells your data to anyone else.

Information we collect

Account information. If you sign in with Apple, Google, or an email address, we receive and store your email and, if you provide it, your name. Sign in with Apple lets you hide your email; if you do, we receive a private relay address. If you don't sign in, the app creates an anonymous account identifier automatically so your data can still sync locally and (if you later sign in) be linked to your real account.

Content you create. Notes, highlights, favorites, scripture links, study collections, and reading plans that you create in the app.

Reading activity. Your reading log, reading streaks, chapter/verse coverage, and aggregate "dwell time" (how long verses are viewed) used to power reading stats and highlight commonly-studied passages.

Study group information. If you use Study Together, we store the groups you belong to and your chosen display name. (Sharing your own notes with a group is a feature that exists in the app but is turned off while we build out content-moderation tools — see "Study Together" below.)

AI feature feedback. If you rate an AI-generated response (thumbs up/down) on a chapter summary, reflection question, word context, or related-verse suggestion, we store that rating along with the passage it relates to and your account identifier, to help us improve prompt quality.

Push notification token. If you enable reminders or group notifications, we store a device push token (issued by Apple/Google via Expo's push service) so we can deliver notifications to your device.

Technical identifiers. An account identifier assigned by our authentication provider (including a temporary anonymous identifier before you sign in), plus basic device/app diagnostics described under "Analytics and crash reporting" below.

We do not collect precise location, contacts, photos, or advertising identifiers.

How we use information

  • To provide the app's features and sync your content across your devices.
  • To power reading statistics, streaks, and study-group features.
  • To generate AI features (chapter summaries, reflection questions, word context, related-verse suggestions) — see "AI features" below for exactly what leaves your device for this.
  • To produce aggregate, non-identifying insights (e.g. which passages are most studied, which features get used) that help improve the app and its content.
  • To operate, secure, diagnose, and troubleshoot the service.

AI features

Cultivate Study's reflection questions, chapter summaries, word context, and related-verse suggestions can be generated two ways:

  • On-device, using Apple Intelligence (SystemLanguageModel) on supported iOS devices. In this mode, nothing related to the request leaves your device.
  • Google Gemini, used when on-device generation isn't available or isn't configured, or for features that specifically use it. In this mode, a prompt built from scripture text and contextual information (such as the book/chapter and any relevant reading context needed to generate a good response) is sent to Google's Generative Language API. We do not send your private notes, highlights, or account identity as part of that prompt. Google's handling of this data is governed by Google's API Terms and Privacy Policy.

Which path is used for a given request may change as the app evolves; we'll update this section if that changes what leaves your device.

Analytics and crash reporting

We use PostHog for two related but distinct purposes:

  • Product analytics — events like which screens are opened, which features are used, and coarse actions (e.g. a chapter was opened, a search was run) so we can understand how the app is used and improve it. These events never include your search text, note or highlight content, or AI-generated text — only metadata (e.g. "a search happened, it returned 4 results," not what you searched for). This is on by default and you can turn it off in Settings → Privacy → Analytics.
  • Crash and error reporting — technical diagnostic data (stack traces, device model/OS version, app version, and the sequence of steps leading up to a crash) so we can find and fix bugs. This is always on and is not affected by the Analytics toggle above, since it's diagnostic data about the app's behavior rather than about you.

Both are tied to your account identifier (anonymous or signed-in) so we can tell how many distinct people are affected by an issue, not to build a profile of you for advertising — we don't use PostHog data for ads, and we don't sell it.

Study Together (groups)

Groups let you track shared reading progress and see a summarized leaderboard of other members' pace (not their raw reading log). Sharing your own notes with a group is implemented in the app but currently turned off — we want real content-moderation tools (reporting, blocking, admin removal) in place before letting group members see each other's free-text notes. If you had shared a note with a group before this was turned off, that share record may still exist in our database but is not currently visible to anyone.

How information is stored and shared

Your data is stored using Supabase, our backend and database provider, which processes data on our behalf. Authentication is handled through Supabase and, if you use them, Sign in with Apple and Sign in with Google. Access to your data is restricted so that you can only read and write your own records (and summarized group progress shared with groups you belong to).

Besides Supabase, the service providers who may process data on our behalf are: Google (Gemini API, for the AI features described above; Google Sign-In, if you use it), Apple (Sign in with Apple, and push notifications on iOS), PostHog (analytics and crash reporting, described above), and Expo (push notification delivery).

We do not sell your personal information. We do not share it with third parties except the service providers above that operate the app, or as required by law.

Your choices and rights

  • Use without an account. You can use core features locally without signing in.
  • Access & sync. Signing in syncs your content to your account.
  • Turn off analytics. Settings → Privacy → Analytics. (Crash reporting stays on — see "Analytics and crash reporting" above.)
  • Turn off notifications. Settings → Reminders / Settings → Groups.
  • Delete your account and data. In the app, go to Settings → Account and choose Delete account. This permanently removes your account and associated data from our servers and clears data on your device. This cannot be undone.

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights (such as access, correction, or deletion). Contact us at cultivatestudy@gmail.com to make a request.

Data retention

We retain your content while your account exists. When you delete your account, your data is deleted from our active systems. Records marked as deleted may persist briefly (up to ~30 days) to allow deletions to propagate across your devices, after which they are purged.

Age and audience

Cultivate Study is rated for ages 12 and up. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has provided us information, contact us and we will delete it.

Security

We use industry-standard measures including encryption in transit and row-level access controls. No method of transmission or storage is 100% secure, but we work to protect your information.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. Material changes will be reflected by updating the effective date above.
