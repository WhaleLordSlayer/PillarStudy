Why trust the connections?

Source first. Then review the graph like it might be wrong.

Explorer data goes through source checks, identity resolution, deterministic validation, semantic review, and adversarial challenge before a candidate can become part of a canonical release.

A source is required.

A canonical fact or relationship needs source support. Model confidence and model agreement do not count as evidence.

Names are resolved conservatively.

Two records are not merged just because the display names match. Occurrences, aliases, chronology, family evidence, and source identifiers are used to keep identities separate.

Being mentioned is different from being present.

Quotations, prophecies, genealogies, and historical references are checked before a person is connected to an event as a participant.

Unknown is allowed.

If the evidence is not strong enough, the graph can leave a date, identity, or relationship unresolved instead of filling the gap with a plausible guess.

A simple example: Moses.

If a New Testament passage mentions Moses, that does not automatically make Moses a participant in the scene. At the Transfiguration he is present, so that connection belongs. In a later discussion that only refers to him, it does not.

Where AI fits

AI helps find candidates, review difficult cases, and challenge proposed changes. It cannot turn its own answer into canonical data. The source evidence remains the deciding factor.

Story-level QA

Does the graph actually tell the story?

Individual connections can all be technically valid and still leave a major story hard to explore. Graph V3 added whole-story audits that check whether important people are connected to the right events and whether major events have the participants needed to make sense.

The latest repair pass worked through those findings before the new graph was promoted.

Release discipline

Explorer ships from a versioned dataset.

Candidate changes stay separate from canonical data until they pass review. Certified releases are deterministic, hashed, and tested so the graph in the app is a specific dataset rather than a live model deciding connections while you read.

Reproducible

Same certified inputs. Same graph.

Sources, IDs, review manifests, and release artifacts are pinned. Certified build steps can verify that repeated builds produce the same canonical output.

Traceable

Connections keep their provenance.

The project tracks what source supports a connection, what rule admitted it, and what QA checked that rule before release.

522
tests passed at the Bible V3 R4 promotion checkpoint

Zero failures were reported. Six tests were intentionally skipped. Release checks also protected historical baselines from being overwritten by the new canonical graph.
